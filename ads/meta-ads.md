Source: the platform-drawbacks section of the landing page. One campaign per platform;
the hook does the audience targeting (only people on that platform feel addressed).

and `noindex` is removed. Ads to a placeholder email waste every dollar.

that can't be sourced.

## Creative direction

Match the landing page's dark section exactly — dark ink green (#1c1e21 on the page),
gold italic serif "Still on WordPress?" as the only large text, small white supporting
line, "Alexander Schottky" wordmark bottom corner. No stock photos, no AI imagery.
The ad should look like nothing else in the feed and identical to the page they land on.
1080x1080 for feed, 1080x1920 for stories/reels. Template: ads/creatives.html.

A second creative to test later: the Slack-conversation mockup from the hero as a
1080x1080 image — it demonstrates the entire service without a word of copy.

## Targeting & budget (starting point)

- Audience: broad US (or start MO + neighboring states to keep CPMs low and it plays to
  "Missouri developer" trust), age 30–65, Advantage+ mostly on. Optionally layer interests:
  small business owners, business page admins. Don't over-narrow — the hook self-selects.
- Structure: 1 campaign per platform, 1 ad set each, 2 text variants per ad set.
- Budget: $10/day per campaign. Run WordPress + orphaned-site + Wix first (biggest
  audiences). Add Squarespace and concrete5 after the first week.
- Judge after 2 weeks / ~$400–600 total: cost per landing-page view, then cost per
  booked call. Kill variants, keep winners. A single door-two client pays for roughly
  a year of testing at this rate.
- UTM tag everything: ?utm_source=meta&utm_campaign=still-on-wordpress etc., so the
  booking form tells you which pain point converts.
