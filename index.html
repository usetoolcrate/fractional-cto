The questions everyone asks

What if we want to edit content ourselves?

In all my years of retainer work, clients have asked me to make the change every single time — that is why they keep me around. Messaging your developer is faster than fighting a page editor.

But if you have genuinely high-frequency content — a jobs board, a news feed, an events calendar — I build that one section as a simple structured editor made for exactly your content. The other 95% of the site stays bulletproof.

What happens if something happens to you?

Everything lives in a plain git repository with documentation, and you own it. Any competent developer can take over tomorrow morning. That is a stronger continuity story than your current CMS, where you are tied to a shrinking pool of specialists — I know, because I am one of them.

Why is there no hourly rate?

Because hours measure my time, not your outcome. You are buying "the site ships on this date" and "changes go live the same day" — so that is what the contract says, and that is what the invoice reads. If I get faster at my job, you should not have to renegotiate; you should just get faster service.

How fast do changes really go live?

The contract guarantees acknowledgment within 2 business hours and most content changes live the same business day. In practice, small changes often ship within the hour. I promise the number I can keep on my worst day and beat it on every other one.

We're not on Concrete CMS. Does this still apply?

Yes. Concrete CMS is my deepest specialty, so migrations off it are where I have the most unfair advantage — but the model works the same from WordPress, Joomla, or an aging custom build. The pitch does not change: fixed price, real deadline, then a developer in your Slack instead of a CMS.

What does the 12-week sprint not include?

The scope is a one-page document we both sign before kickoff: which pages, which integrations, what gets migrated. Anything outside it goes on the next month's outcome list instead of silently inflating the project. That discipline is why I can put a real date on delivery.
